Test build

The seven steps behind this page

Each prompt run against BoomShare's own content. Outputs are collapsed — this is the working, not the site.

1 · The design direction⌄
Pure black and off-white full-bleed panels, one idea per screen, extreme vertical whitespace, everything centred. Type is the system face — on a Mac this page renders in SF Pro, so the headlines are the real thing rather than an approximation. Weight 600, never 800; tracking tightens to −0.035em as size grows. One accent only: BoomShare coral, used for links and the single kicker per panel, so the brand survives the minimalism. Product photography is a single machined object on black with one rim light — the inverse of the cartoon illustrations on the live site.

Deliberately different from the live page

  • No grain, no blush glows, no floating illustrations
  • No interactive demo in the hero — the hero is one sentence and one object
  • Section rhythm roughly doubled: 150px versus 76px
2 · The first thing visitors see⌄
Visitor arrives from a Meta ad on a phone, mid-scroll, having just been reminded that a client owes them money. One promise matters: they will not have to ask. Evidence above the fold is the object itself — a lock, unmistakably. The action inside ten seconds is a tap on a text link, not a form.

Three headlines worth testing against each other

A · Get paid before they download.Plain, mechanical, states the trade. Currently live on this build.
B · The files open when the money lands.Describes the product rather than the outcome. Truer, slightly slower.
C · Nobody has to ask twice.Emotional, no mechanism. Highest ceiling, highest risk of being ignored.

Primary and secondary

Primary is a chevron link to the lock demonstration, not to signup — the visitor is not ready at second three. Signup is the secondary, phrased as the price: “Free until you're paid.”
3 · How the site moves⌄
The brand's personality is calm and slightly withholding, so motion is slow, few, and always in service of the lock.

Entry

Hero elements rise 26px and resolve from a 6px blur over 800ms on a decelerating curve, staggered 90ms apart. Blur-to-sharp is the one signature — it rhymes with the watermarked preview.

The one orchestrated moment

A 300vh sticky section where scroll progress crossfades the closed lock into the open one. At 45% the amount fades in and the headline swaps from “One tap.” to “Paid.” Nothing else on the page animates on scroll position — spending the whole motion budget in one place is what makes it read as intentional.

Hover and press

Chevrons nudge 3px. Buttons brighten 8%. No lift, no shadow, no scale on cards.

Never moves

The navigation, all prices, the fee numbers, the legal line. Nothing that a visitor might be trying to read carefully is allowed to animate.

Mobile budget

The sticky moment collapses to a static panel under 900px in reduced-motion, images are capped at 1600px, and every transition is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.
4 · The copywriting⌄
Awareness on arrival is problem-aware, solution-unaware: they know chasing is miserable, they do not know a lock exists as a product. So the first job is naming the mechanism, not the benefit.

Objection by scroll depth

  • Hero — “what is this?” → name the mechanism in one sentence.
  • After the lock — “isn't that rude?” → show the client's side as one calm tap.
  • Preview — “how do they judge it?” → they see everything.
  • Tiles — “what about revisions?” → same link, price unchanged.
  • Pricing — “what's the catch?” → the client is never charged.

Their words, not ours

Freelancers say chasing, ghosted, they went quiet, I hate asking. They do not say accounts receivable, cashflow or escrow. None of those three words appear on this page.

Microcopy

Buttons name the outcome: “Start free”, “Go Pro”, “Get paid for free”. The final line under pricing is a trust statement, not a sales one.
5 · The build plan⌄

Components that repeat

  • Panel — four surfaces: black, near-black, white, grey
  • Display / Headline / Sub / Body — the whole type scale
  • ChevronLink, PillButton
  • Tile — light or dark, optional full-width span
  • StickyMoment — two images plus scroll progress
  • PlanCard, Disclosure

Content that changes often

Headline variants, prices and caps, the partner examples, and every figure in the fee line. All of it belongs in one content file, never inline in components.

Breakpoints

620 (two-up numbers stack) · 760 (tiles and nav collapse) · 900 (sticky moment simplifies) · 1020 (content max width).

Loading

System font first so there is no swap on Apple devices; one Google fallback for everyone else. Hero image eager, all others lazy. Images capped at 1600px and served as JPEG at 84 — the three on this page total roughly 450 KB.

Pre-launch checklist

  • Every interactive target ≥ 44px
  • Contrast checked on both black and grey panels
  • Reduced-motion collapses the sticky section
  • No horizontal scroll at 390px
  • Focus visible on every link
6 · The conversion audit⌄
Path: ad → hero → lock moment → preview → pricing → signup. Friction is concentrated in two places.

The three highest-impact fixes, in order

1 · There is no working demo above the foldThe live site lets you actually pay a delivery page; this build only describes it. That interaction is almost certainly the single strongest asset either page has, and this design drops it for the sake of restraint. Expected impact: large and negative. Fix: put the payable phone inside the sticky moment.
2 · No proof of any kindNo testimonials, no counts, no logos. Fine for Apple, who need no introduction; fatal for an unknown product asking for payment details. Fix: one honest line above pricing once the Phase 0 pilot has real numbers.
3 · The primary action is a chevron linkElegant, and it under-converts against a filled button on cold paid traffic. Fix: A/B it directly — this is the cheapest test on the page.

Questions left unanswered before the button

How long does setup take; do I need a company; what happens if the client refuses to pay. The live page answers all three in its FAQ; this build has no FAQ at all, which is a real loss.

The metric

Landing → signup, then signup → Stripe connected. The spec's bars are ≥4% and ≥50%. Nothing else on this page matters until those two move.
7 · The launch plan⌄

First thirty days

Week 1 — ship to the ten Phase 0 users only, no ad spend, watch whether real locked links actually get sent. Week 2 — open €2–3k of Meta to Spain and Brazil at phone placements, conversion event set to payment completed. Weeks 3–4 — read cost per paying user against the €60 bar and the 40% second-paid-link bar at day 30.

Three experiments, in priority order

  • Headline A versus C — mechanism against emotion
  • Chevron link against filled button as the primary
  • This Apple build against the current illustrated one, straight split

Cheap feedback

Session recordings on the first 200 sessions, and one WhatsApp question to every pilot user after their first paid link: “what nearly stopped you sending it?”

When to stop changing

Freeze the page once landing → signup clears 4% across 1,000 sessions. Below that, keep testing the hero only; above it, every further change should be to the product, not the page.
