Open questions for counsel

Version 0.1 · 17 September 2026 · BoomShare for Freelancers

Decisions that must be made before these documents can be finalised. Each one changes the text.

1 · Which company operates the service — and it is not settled today

This is the blocker. The existing boomshare.ai terms say the operator is Albert D Matalon, LLC, registered in California, under California law with binding arbitration and a class-action waiver. The freelancer page says Mazalore Ltd. Those cannot both be right, and neither may be the right answer here.

Three separate roles need an entity assigned, and they need not be the same one:

RoleWhat it means
Platform operator / data controllerSigns these terms with freelancers, named in the privacy notice, answers to the CNPD or AEPD.
Stripe Connect platformHolds the Stripe platform account, signs Stripe's Connect Platform Agreement, collects application fees. Stripe will underwrite this entity.
Merchant of record for ProSells the €19/€149 subscription, appears on card statements, handles those refunds. Mazalore's existing role fits here.

Note the tension: Mazalore is recorded as a merchant of record — it charges cards on behalf of another company. The whole promise of this product is the opposite: your money never touches our account, because charges are direct to the freelancer's own Stripe account. Mazalore is therefore a natural fit for the Pro subscription and a poor fit for the delivery payments. Keeping those two flows in different entities is defensible, but it must be described accurately in the terms.

A California entity with California law and arbitration is also a weak fit for a product launching to freelancers in Portugal, Spain and Brazil, where mandatory consumer and small-trader protections cannot be contracted away.

2 · Stripe Connect specifics

  • Which Connect flavour — Express or Standard? It changes who owes the freelancer disclosures and who handles disputes.
  • Confirm we are structured as direct charges with an application fee, not destination charges or separate charges and transfers. Only direct charges support the claim that funds never touch us.
  • Stripe requires platforms to surface its Connected Account Agreement during onboarding — confirm the flow does.
  • Pix and MB Way availability on a Connect platform for each target country.

3 · Tax

  • VAT treatment of our commission to EU freelancers — reverse charge where they are VAT-registered, and what happens when they are not.
  • Whether OSS registration is needed for Pro subscriptions sold to non-business users.
  • Brazil: whether selling to Brazilian freelancers creates a local tax or registration obligation, and how BRL pricing is handled.

4 · Consumer status

The terms say BoomShare is for people acting professionally, which keeps most consumer law out. That will not hold for every sole trader in every country. Counsel should confirm what applies anyway in Portugal, Spain and Brazil — particularly withdrawal rights on Pro and the enforceability of the liability cap.

5 · Hosting intermediary status

We host files other people upload. Confirm how the EU Digital Services Act applies at our size — notice and action mechanism, a published point of contact, and transparency obligations — and whether the light regime for micro and small enterprises applies.

6 · Data protection housekeeping

  • Whether a DPO must be appointed, and an encarregado for Brazil.
  • An EU representative under Art. 27 if the operating entity is outside the EEA — likely required if a California company operates the service.
  • Completing the subprocessor list: hosting, email, analytics, error monitoring and the language-model provider.
  • Whether processing delivery contents needs a DPIA, given some deliveries will contain third-party personal data.

7 · The AI reminders

Confirm that "drafted by a model, approved and sent by the freelancer" keeps us clear of automated-decision rules, and that sending as the freelancer is compatible with WhatsApp Business policy and with ePrivacy rules on unsolicited messages.

8 · Things to fix on the page itself

  • A cookie consent banner is now on the page and must be wired to actually gate analytics and advertising scripts before any are added.
  • The testimonials are written examples, marked as such. They must be replaced with real, attributable quotes — or removed — before any paid traffic runs.
  • The fee comparison names Fiverr, HoneyBook and PayPal. Check the figures are current and that comparative advertising rules are met in each market.
